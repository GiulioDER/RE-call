# Pre-registration: can the memo's own AUTHORING modality make it discoverable?

**Date:** 2026-08-27   **Status:** predicted, not yet measured
**Probe:** `scripts/agent_ab_prepare_discoverability_corpus.py` +
`scripts/agent_ab_probe_discoverability.py`, committed with this record.

## The question

If every memo in the frozen benchmark corpus is authored for the future searcher (a
searcher-oriented title and description, a "you need this when" section at the top, or a separate
pointer document, each carrying task-intent vocabulary generated from the memo text by a strong
model), do the recorded queries of `agent-ab-skill-001`'s miss sessions retrieve their governing
memo at top-5, without breaking the sessions that hit?

## Why this direction, stated before any number

Three measured facts point here and none of them settles it:

1. Instruction text solved WHETHER agents search (46/46) and did not move HOW they search: the
   queries stay in goal vocabulary and `ts-lf-rewrite` missed 6 of 6
   (`docs/preregistrations/2026-08-22-hazard-query-instruction.md`).
2. Query-side expansion rescued 3 of 15 against a build bar of 5, because a rewriter at query time
   holds only what the agent held (`docs/preregistrations/2026-08-23-decompose-expansion-probe.md`).
3. Index-side aliases APPENDED AT THE BOTTOM of each memo, generated by haiku, measured a within
   instrument 46 of 46 no-change (probe void by apparatus gate,
   `docs/preregistrations/2026-08-23-alias-index-probe.md`). That record names two undecided
   mechanisms (the appended section merges into an existing chunk and dilutes; uniform augmentation
   cancels in the ranking) and two unexplored variants: separate alias documents, and a stronger
   generator.

This probe is the licensed follow-up: both named variants, plus the placement the dilution
mechanism predicts matters (the TOP of the memo, beside title and description, rather than the
bottom), with the generation reused identically across arms so structure is isolated from content.
The deliverable, if any arm works, is an authoring guideline (how a session writing a memo should
choose its title and shape), not retrieval surgery.

## Design

- **Frozen sources, recovered exactly as the alias probe recovered them**: the 194 sources of
  generation `gen_f01fc522` from the evidence corpus `recall-agentab-corpus` (127.0.0.1:5407,
  read-only), live file used only when its sha256 matches the recorded `source_sha256`, drifted
  files reconstructed from chunks with the learned joiner and flagged.
- **One generation pass, three assemblies.** For each non-index memo, one call
  (`anthropic/claude-sonnet-5` via OpenRouter, temperature 0, fixed prompt committed inside the
  preparation script) returns `{title, description, tasks[5]}` written from the future searcher's
  point of view. The same triple is then assembled three ways, so the arms differ only in
  structure:
  - **retitle**: generated title inserted as the first body line, frontmatter `description:`
    replaced by the generated one. Everything else verbatim.
  - **restructured**: retitle, plus a `## You need this when` section holding the 5 task phrasings
    immediately after the title, before the original body.
  - **pointer**: source verbatim, plus a separate document `<stem>--tasks.md` holding the title,
    the 5 task phrasings, and a pointer to the memo. Because the pointer stem contains the memo
    stem, the existing substring outcome check counts a pointer retrieval as reach; direct-only
    reach is also recorded.
- **Uniform application.** Every non-index memo is treated, never only the governing ones, because
  the deployed form of an authoring modality is corpus-wide, and because uniform augmentation is
  one of the two mechanisms this probe exists to decide.
- ⛔ **The generator sees only memo text.** The preparation script structurally cannot read the
  benchmark archive; every generated triple is recorded verbatim in `rewrites.json` for audit. One
  prompt, one run, one verdict: no rewording queries, no iterating the prompt against the result.
- **Four rebuilds in this session's own container** (never the evidence volume), same committed
  builder, chunker, embedder (`fastembed`) and calibration query set:  `probe_control`,
  `probe_retitle`, `probe_restructured`, `probe_pointer`, in separate databases, built
  sequentially under the standing embedding bounds (`RECALL_FASTEMBED_BATCH=16`,
  `RECALL_INDEX_BATCH_CHUNKS=64`, no concurrent embedding process, checked before starting). The
  builds run on this workstation because the evidence corpus and the replay transport live here,
  as the alias probe's builds did; the corpus is a local benchmark artifact, not a served corpus.
- **Replay**: the recorded `recall_search` queries of the admitted on-arm `memory_only` sessions
  of `agent-ab-skill-001`, over the same stdio transport, same top-5, against all four rebuilds.
  Session outcome: governing memo in top-5 for any of the session's queries.

## Registered exclusion, and the rewritten apparatus gate

The `ts-raise-on-missing` family (1 miss, 5 hits) is **excluded by this registration**, exactly as
the void record's follow-up note prescribes: its governing memo
`missing-input-becomes-a-clean-null` is the single governing source among the 20 reconstructions,
the chunker transforms text so no reconstruction is bit-exact, and all five hits the last control
rebuild lost were this family. Its sessions are replayed and recorded but read from no endpoint.

That leaves **40 sessions: 14 misses** (`ts-lf-rewrite` 6, `ts-worktree-import` 5,
`ts-sample-covers-tail` 3) **and 26 hits**.

**Apparatus gate, which VOIDS the probe:** the control rebuild must reproduce at least 13 of 14
misses AND at least 24 of 26 hits. The last rebuild scored 14 of 14 and 26 of 26 on exactly this
subset, so a shortfall means the instrument changed and no verdict is read from any treatment arm.

## What I predict

Ceiling arithmetic first, stated separately per the standing correction in
`i-over-predict-effect-magnitudes`: the ceiling is 14 of 14, this is a DESCRIPTION-side
intervention (it changes how content is written, not a decision the system makes), so the
quarter-to-half discount applies to the fraction of misses whose recorded queries share any
bridgeable vocabulary with a well-authored surface, which I estimate at most half of them.

| # | Prediction | Falsified if |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Mechanism ordering: pointer >= restructured >= retitle on rescue.** Separate documents escape both suspected null mechanisms; top-placement escapes one; a title alone adds the least new surface | retitle rescues strictly more than pointer |
| 2 | **Best arm rescue: 3 to 6 of 14** | 2 or fewer (no better than the query-side floor), or 7 and above (I under-called a working mechanism) |
| 3 | **`ts-lf-rewrite`: at least 1 of 6 in the best arm.** The memo says "scripting an edit in Python" and "editing repo files"; a searcher-oriented title derived from that text can land near "version bump script" queries without ever saying "version" | 0 of 6 |
| 4 | **Retention: retitle and restructured >= 25 of 26; pointer >= 23 of 26.** Pointer adds ~190 goal-vocabulary documents competing for five slots, so it pays the largest retention price | any arm below its bound |
| 5 | **Cost, by arithmetic not hope: the whole probe under 3 hours wall clock and under 5 USD.** ~190 generation calls at ~10 s serial-equivalent, four builds at ~10 to 15 minutes each sequentially, four stdio replays of ~78 distinct queries | either bound exceeded |

## Decision rule, full partition

Let the qualifying arms be those with retention at or above 24 of 26, and read rescue from the
best qualifying arm:

- **Rescue >= 7**: the authoring modality works. Write the memo-authoring guideline (title,
  description, top section) into the session-close path, and preregister a live agent A/B before
  any claim about agent-level effect.
- **Rescue 4 to 6**: mechanism real but weak. Adopt the title and description modality as writing
  guidance (it costs nothing at write time), no retrieval surgery from this lane, and one further
  registered iteration on authoring design is licensed.
- **Rescue 3 or fewer**: write-side authoring does not bridge the formulation gap at current
  generator strength, completing the triangle (query-side 3/15, bottom-append 0, authored surface
  <= 3): the gap is then a retrieval-side problem (lexical or hybrid scoring, or interactive
  search), and no fourth generation-side variant is licensed without new mechanism evidence.
- **No arm qualifies on retention**: the modality is net harmful at corpus scale regardless of
  rescue, and the record says so.
- **Apparatus gate failed**: VOID, no verdict from any arm, mismatch investigated under a new
  record.

## What I already know

- The 15 misses, 31 hits, their queries and governing memos: archive
  `~/.claude/archive/agent-ab-skill-001/`, summarized in the alias probe's artifact.
- Query-side expansion rescued 3 of 15; bottom-appended haiku aliases moved 0 of 46 sessions
  within a void instrument; both records above.
- The stated prior in the void record is LOW for prompt-generated vocabulary bridging this gap,
  and this probe is run anyway because it is the exact pair of variants that record reserved, and
  because the deliverable on success is an authoring guideline this project wants regardless.

## Confounds I can name now

1. **Author contamination, disclosed.** I have seen, quoted in memos and in an archive listing,
   several recorded queries: three baseline-run `ts-lf-rewrite` queries, and the head of the skill
   run's query file (the `ts-false-zero-search` family, a hit family, plus two `ts-lf-rewrite`
   entries). The generator cannot see them (structural), and the fixed prompt is generic, but the
   prompt's author is not blind, exactly as in the two prior records.
2. **Calibration differs per rebuild.** Ranks decide the endpoints, not verdicts; the apparatus
   gate bounds what rebuild differences can hide.
3. **Replacing the description can lose retrieving vocabulary.** Retitle and restructured replace
   the frontmatter description; if an existing hit depended on it, retention pays. That is a real
   property of the modality, not an artifact, and retention is registered to watch it.
4. **The pointer arm changes corpus size** (roughly 194 to 384 documents), so its ranking
   competition differs globally from the other arms. That is also the deployed form of the
   modality, and the retention bound prices it.
5. **The generator model is not the memo's author.** The deployed modality would be applied by the
   session that lived the failure, with more context than the memo text. A generated-from-text
   probe UNDERSTATES that author, so a positive result transfers, and a null leaves author-written
   memos formally untested.

## Result (2026-08-27)

**Status: measured, and the probe is VALID: the apparatus gate passed cleanly.** Control rebuild
reproduced **14 of 14** non-excluded misses (gate >= 13) and **26 of 26** non-excluded hits
(gate >= 24). Control chunk count 1,019 against the original 1,006 and the prior rebuild's 1,016;
25 sources reconstructed this time against 20 then (the live store keeps drifting), and the only
governing memo among them is the excluded family's, exactly as registered.

**Measured: rescue 0 of 14, in every arm. Retention 26 of 26, in every arm.**

| arm | rescue | rescue direct | `ts-lf-rewrite` | retention |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| retitle | 0/14 | 0/14 | 0/6 | 26/26 |
| restructured | 0/14 | 0/14 | 0/6 | 26/26 |
| pointer | 0/14 | 0/14 | 0/6 | 26/26 |

Artifact: `benchmarks/artifacts/agent_ab/discoverability-probe/` (rewrites.json holds all 190
generated triples verbatim; discoverability-probe.json holds every retrieved top-5).

**The instrument was live, which is what makes this null informative.** Post-hoc diagnostics,
labelled as such: per arm, 72 to 77 of the 78 distinct queries returned a DIFFERENT top-5 than
control, and in the pointer arm the `--tasks` documents appear in top-5 for **67 of 78** queries.
So the treatment was present, retrievable, and reranking the corpus; it still rescued nothing.

**Why, visible in the retrievals:** for "version bump release script" the pointer arm's top-5 is
led by `recall-public-distribution--tasks.md`, a memo genuinely ABOUT releases. Uniform
goal-vocabulary surfaces make goal queries retrieve the goal-adjacent memos' surfaces, and the
governing memo loses to documents that are legitimately closer to the stated goal. That is dense
retrieval working correctly. The link from "bump the version" to "a script will write a file with
CRLF" is a causal chain, not a similarity, and no wording of the CRLF memo makes it more about
version bumping than the memos that are about version bumping, in a corpus where every memo now
speaks goal vocabulary.

**Predictions against measurements:**

| # | predicted | measured | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | pointer >= restructured >= retitle | 0 = 0 = 0 | vacuously satisfied, uninformative |
| 2 | best arm rescue 3 to 6 of 14 | 0 of 14 | **falsified, too high again** |
| 3 | `ts-lf-rewrite` >= 1 of 6 | 0 of 6 | **falsified** |
| 4 | retention: retitle, restructured >= 25; pointer >= 23 | 26, 26, 26 | confirmed, and the predicted pointer damage never came |
| 5 | under 3 hours and under 5 USD | ~1h50m of wall clock across attempts, ~1 USD | confirmed |

Prediction 2 extends the pattern in `i-over-predict-effect-magnitudes` a third time for
wording-side interventions: the quarter-to-half discount was applied and the measurement still
came in at zero, below the discounted band. Three consecutive wording-side results (appended
aliases, query expansion, authored surfaces) have now landed at or under the bottom of their
bands.

**Decision, per the registered partition: rescue <= 3, so write-side authoring does not bridge
the formulation gap, and the triangle is complete.** Query-side expansion 3/15, bottom-appended
generated aliases 0 effect, and now authored searcher-oriented surfaces 0/14 across three
structural variants with a stronger generator and a passing apparatus gate. No fourth
generation-side variant is licensed. The formulation gap is a RELEVANCE problem, not a vocabulary
problem: the governing memo is often not the semantically closest document to the goal query, and
cannot be made so without displacing documents that answer the goal better. What remains is
retrieval-side (bridging the task-to-hazard causal link at query time with corpus knowledge, or
lexical/hybrid scoring where an exact trigger term exists) or interactive search behaviour, and a
proposal in either direction needs its own registration.

**One registered confound survives and is sharpened, not resolved:** the generator saw only memo
text (confound 5). The one query term that would have bridged `ts-lf-rewrite` is "version", and it
appears nowhere in the memo because the ORIGIN session was doing scripted refactors, not version
bumps. But this also bounds the true-author ceiling: the origin author would have written
"refactor scripts", which bridges the recorded version-bump queries no better. When the future
tasks that strike a hazard are semantically diverse, no finite task list written at authoring time
covers them, and that is now an argument from mechanism, not just a null.
